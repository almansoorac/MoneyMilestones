Average salary in Australia: where you really sit

What counts as a good salary in Australia? Here is the median, the average, income by percentile, and pay by industry — plus a quick check of where your salary ranks.

MM By the MoneyMilestones team ·Updated 2 July 2026

"What is the average salary in Australia?" sounds simple, but the honest answer is: it depends which average you mean, and whether you count everyone or just full-time workers. Here is where real Australian incomes actually sit, and how to find your own place in the picture.

Median vs average — why they differ

The median (the middle earner) is around $56,000 across all taxpayers, closer to $62,000 for employees, and about $84,000 for full-time workers. The average full-time salary is higher — roughly $100,000 — because a small number of very high earners pull the mean up. That is why the median is usually the more honest guide to what a typical Australian earns.

What it means for your take-home

A higher salary does not translate dollar-for-dollar into your bank account, because Australia's progressive tax system taxes each slice of income at a higher rate. Someone on $100,000 sits in the 30% bracket but pays an average rate closer to 20% — so their take-home is higher than the headline rate suggests.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average salary in Australia?

The average full-time salary is around $100,000 a year, but that is pulled up by high earners. The median (middle) full-time salary is closer to $84,000, and across all taxpayers the median is about $56,000 — a better guide to what a typical person earns.

What salary puts me in the top 10%?

Roughly $150,000 a year puts you in about the top 10% of Australian earners. The top 5% starts near $180,000, and the top 1% around $420,000. These are approximate figures from ATO income data.

Is the median or the average more useful?

The median, in most cases. Because incomes are skewed by a small number of very high earners, the average overstates what a typical person earns. The median — the middle value — better reflects a normal income.
